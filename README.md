#  Evaluation of Bias in Greek Large Language Models (LLMs)

##  Overview

This thesis focuses on the **evaluation of bias in Greek Large Language Models (LLMs)**.  
In recent years, the field of **Natural Language Processing (NLP)** has rapidly evolved, making LLMs a core component of numerous applications such as machine translation, text generation, and sentiment analysis.  

Among these, **Masked Language Models (MLMs)** hold particular importance, as they are widely used for pre-training and fine-tuning downstream NLP tasks. However, MLMs may **encode social biases** during training due to imbalanced or biased data sources, which can negatively impact fairness and reliability in real-world applications.

While extensive research has been conducted on bias evaluation in **English-based models**, there is a noticeable **lack of studies for the Greek language**.  
The aim of this thesis is to **address this research gap** by evaluating well-known **Greek language models** for **gender-related bias**.

---

##  Objectives

The main objectives of this thesis are:
- To **evaluate gender bias** in Greek LLMs through quantitative and qualitative analysis.  
- To **translate and adapt the HONEST benchmark** into Greek, ensuring linguistic and cultural consistency.  
- To apply **bias evaluation metrics** such as **DisCo** and **HONEST**, assessing both gender correlations and toxicity in model predictions.  
- To contribute towards **fairer and more transparent NLP applications** in Greek.

---

##  Methodology

The study evaluates three widely used Greek MLMs:

- **GreekBERT**  
- **XLM-RoBERTa**  
- **Greek SocialBERT**

The evaluation employs the **Greek-translated HONEST benchmark**, which measures:
- **HONEST Metric:** Percentage of toxic or harmful words generated by the model.  
- **DisCo Metric (Distributional Correlation):** Measures correlations in model predictions that reveal potential gender bias.

Each model was tested under controlled experimental settings to ensure reproducibility and comparability. The combined use of these metrics provides a reliable indication of **how social biases manifest** in Greek language models.

---

##  Results and Findings

The experimental results reveal that:
- Despite their **strong linguistic performance**, Greek MLMs exhibit **clear signs of gender bias**.  
- Certain occupations, adjectives, and social roles are disproportionately associated with specific genders.  
- The observed patterns align with trends found in international studies, confirming that **bias in language models is a universal phenomenon**.  

These findings emphasize the need for **further research and bias mitigation strategies**, especially in low-resource languages like Greek.

---

##  Contribution

This work represents the **first systematic study** of bias in Greek LLMs using standardized benchmarks.  
It contributes to the **ethical development of AI** by:
- Highlighting existing gender stereotypes embedded in Greek models.  
- Providing a reproducible experimental setup for future studies.  
- Encouraging the design of **debiasing methods** and **fair evaluation frameworks** for Greek NLP.
